BE PREPARED BEFORE HEADING OUT<br>BEFORE HEADING OUT<br>Ride a bike that fits you—if it’s too big, it’s harder to control<br>FIT BIKE<br>the bike.<br>PRE-RIDE CHECK<br>Ride a bike that works—it really doesn’t matter how well<br>you ride if the brakes don’t work.<br>Wear equipment to protect you and make you more visible to others, like a bike helmet,<br>bright clothing (during the day), reflective gear, and a white front light and red rear light<br>and reflectors on your bike (at night, or when visibility is poor).<br>Ride one per seat, with both hands on the handlebars, unless signaling a turn.<br>Carry all items in a backpack or strapped to the back of the bike.<br>Tuck and tie your shoe laces and pant legs so they don’t get caught in your bike chain.<br>Plan your route—if driving as a vehicle on the road, choose routes with less traffic and<br>slower speeds. Your safest route may be away from traffic altogether, in a bike lane or on<br>a bike path.<br><!-- End of picture text -->

DRIVE DEFENSIVELY - FOCUSED AND ALERT<br>Be focused and alert to the road and all traffic around you; anticipate what others may do, before they<br>do it. This is defensive driving—the quicker you notice a potential conflict, the quicker you can act to<br>avoid a potential crash:<br>Drive with the flow, in the same direction as traffic.<br>Obey street signs, signals, and road markings, just like a car.<br>Assume the other person doesn’t see you; look ahead for hazards or situations to avoid<br>that may cause you to fall, like toys, pebbles, potholes, grates, train tracks.<br>No texting, listening to music or using anything that distracts you by taking your eyes and<br>ears or your mind off the road and traffic.<br><!-- End of picture text -->
