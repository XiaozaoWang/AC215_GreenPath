## Key points

- People who exercise on hot days are more likely to become dehydrated and get heat-related illness.
- Take steps to protect your health and the health of others on hot days.
- If you feel faint or weak, STOP all activity and get to a cool place.

## Stay safe on hot days

**If you plan to exercise while it's hot outside:**

- Limit outdoor activity, especially during the middle of the day when the sun is hottest if possible.
- Wear and reapply sunscreen as indicated on the package.
- Schedule workouts and practices earlier or later in the day when the temperature is cooler.
- Pace activity. Start activities slow and pick up the pace gradually.
- Drink more water than usual, and don't wait until you're thirsty to drink more. Muscle cramping may be an early sign of heat-related illness.
- Monitor a teammate's condition, and have someone do the same for you.
- Wear loose, lightweight, light-colored clothing.
- Follow [additional tips](/heat-health/about/index.html) on how to prevent heat-related illness.

**Learn how to spot heat-related illness.**

- Seek medical care immediately if you or a teammate has [symptoms of heat-related illness](/heat-health/about/index.html).
- Take a CDC training course. [Learn more](https://www.cdc.gov/nceh/hsb/extreme/heat_illness_training.htm) on how to spot heat-related illness by participating in this course designed for coaches, teachers, parents, and high school athletes.

**Check your local HeatRisk and Air Quality**

Enter your zip code on the [CDC HeatRisk Dashboard](http://www.cdc.gov/heatrisk) for the heat risk forecast in your area and recommended actions to protect your health.

- Plus, check air quality levels – so you can plan your week with your health in mind.
